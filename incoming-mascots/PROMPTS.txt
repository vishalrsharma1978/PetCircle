    same aspect as cat1.png (1024x768)  •  same model (GPT Image 2)

NEVER SAY "SEAMLESS"
--------------------
"Seamless" is the photographic term for a studio backdrop roll — a paper
sweep with a floor curve. Asking for a "plain pure white background (RGB 255,255,255), cut-out style, no studio backdrop, no floor surface"
invites a grey studio scene with a visible floor and a cast shadow, which
is the opposite of a cut-out. The first dog came back exactly that way and
could not be keyed: its brightest fur (226) was lighter than the darkest
background (213), so no threshold separates them, and the floor plane
survives every flood fill. Say "isolated, cut-out style, RGB 255 white,
no floor surface" instead.


NO SHADOWS — THIS ONE IS NOT OPTIONAL
-------------------------------------
Every prompt says "NO shadow and NO reflection". A generated contact shadow
is dark enough to land inside the animal's own brightness range, so nothing
can separate the two — the shadow survives the cutout as a grey skirt round
the feet. Invisible on a white page, obvious the moment the animal sits on
the cream diorama or the dark den background. The scene casts its own
shadow, so a baked-in one was never wanted.


WHY THE POSE WORDING MATTERS
----------------------------
These get cut into layers so they can animate. Two parts get cut:

  TAIL  -> wags.  Painted BEHIND the body, so a ragged cut at the tail
                  root is hidden. This ONLY works if the tail is clearly
                  clear of the body in the photo — a tail tucked behind
                  the animal or curled across its chest cannot be cut out
                  without taking a bite out of the body with it. Hence
                  "held clear of the body" in every prompt below.

  HEAD  -> tilts. Painted OVER the body, so the neck join is covered.
                  Needs the head held up with a bit of neck showing.

Everything else stays one flat layer. Ears and blinking are deliberately
not attempted: they sit in front with nothing to hide the join, so a
rotated cut-out ear just exposes the original ear underneath it.

Rabbit and Small Pet have no usable tail — they get the head tilt only,
which is why their wording doesn't stress tail separation.
